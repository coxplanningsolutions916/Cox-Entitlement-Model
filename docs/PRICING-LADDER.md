# Lifetime payment schedule for the model

Revised 1 October 2026 (second pass) to Chris's direction: **the DDL steps stay as they are**, and the results of every step feed the same model. Step 1a is the screening step with three rungs (Screening, Roadmap, Roadmap Plus); Step 1b is the Initial Site Review, the first field task order (site surveys and data collection) and the step most Roadmaps are expected to convert to. **Step 2 is conceptual design and alternatives, with engineering front and center**, so the client sees a project take shape before anyone talks about permit preparation and processing, which is Step 4. Steps map to the Productive phases: 1b = 01 Assessment, 2 = 02 Design, 3 = 03 Planning, 4 = 04 Permits, 5 = 05 Compliance. The Screening fee pays for setting the model up and the initial review; the Roadmap fee pays for additional review and the recommendation; there are **no credits between rungs**; from Step 1b on, the model's upkeep is inside the project coordination fee on every project; the model publishes to the client dashboard from Step 1a. The rule behind it: **the client pays for each depth once, never re-pays for a fact already established, and every later fee carries the model's upkeep inside it.**

## Roadmap Plus in detail

**When it applies** (any one of these, decided by the scale test the model runs during the Screening and the planner confirms):

1. More than one parcel, or parcels in more than one jurisdiction.
2. Mapped waters, wetlands, flood hazard, or listed-species habitat on or adjacent to the site, so that the resource-permit stack is in play.
3. The stated use needs a discretionary entitlement (rezone, general plan amendment, specific plan amendment, use permit on a contested use) or annexation.
4. The client needs a package for a board, an investment committee, a lender, or a public agency, whether or not the site is complex.

**What the extra $2,500 and three staff hours buy, beyond the Roadmap scope:**

| Addition | Who | Hours | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sub-consultant desk read | Senior biologist, or engineer where infrastructure or flood is the issue | 2 | A specialist reads the Screening's constraints and the planner's path and writes a half-page opinion: what is likely real, what is likely mapped noise, what field work would settle it, and the season window that governs it |
| Scenario comparison | Senior planner (within the 4) | included | Two or three development scenarios (the client's program, the HBU alternative, a by-right fallback) run through the model side by side: approvals, schedule, cost range, confidence, for each |
| Board-ready summary | Senior planner (within the 4) | included | A two-page summary and a ten-slide deck in the client's format: the decision, the scenarios, the five assumptions that matter, the verification plan and its cost |
| Second session | Principal | 1 | A presentation to the board or committee, with questions, in place of a memo they read alone |
| Multi-parcel handling | Model | — | Each parcel screened, then the assemblage read as one site, with the jurisdiction split shown |

What it is not: no field work, no agency meetings, no studies, no pro forma. Those are Step 1b and later, and the Roadmap Plus scope says so.

## The client dashboard tie

From Step 1a the client has a dashboard link (the existing Client Links page on the Cox dashboard, extended). It shows the current roadmap (approvals, schedule, season windows), the program budget with confidence by line, the assumption register with what is verified and what is open, the change log, and the next step with its price. Every model refresh republishes it. At Step 1b and later the same page becomes the client statement already in use (hours, invoiced, paid, percent complete by deliverable), so the client sees the plan and the money on one page. The model writes one JSON per project into the dashboard's data folder the same way the budgets snapshot does today.

## What "inside the project coordination fee" means in a scope

Every task order's coordination line reads, in substance: *Project coordination (10% of labor) includes maintaining the project Roadmap model: monthly schedule and budget refresh, assumption register updates as deliverables land, change notices for rule or map changes affecting the site, and the client dashboard and statement.* That sentence makes the model a billed, expected part of every project rather than a free extra.

## Rules

1. One price per depth, no credits. A free launch Screening is a Screening; the client who proceeds pays the full Roadmap.
2. A second site for the same client is a new Screening unless it is part of the same assemblage.
3. Re-screening a stale Screening (more than 12 months old, or the jurisdiction changed its rules) is a new Screening.
4. No fee ever re-establishes a fact the model already holds. If a later task order would re-do work, the register says so and the scope excludes it.
5. The Roadmap always ends with a priced Step 1b proposal; the proposal is part of the $2,500, not a separate sales step.
6. The market section ships in every Screening, including the free ones.
7. The results of every step, from the free intake screen to Step 5, feed the same model. Nothing is re-keyed.
