# Lifetime payment schedule for the model

Revised 1 October 2026 to Chris's direction: Step 1a pays for setting the model up and the initial review; Step 1b pays for additional review; there are **no credits between steps**; from Step 2 on the model's upkeep is inside the project coordination fee on every project; the model publishes to the client dashboard from Step 1a onward. The rule behind it: **the client pays for each depth once, never re-pays for a fact already established, and every later fee carries the model's upkeep inside it.**

## Step 1b Plus in detail

**When it applies** (any one of these, decided by the scale test the model runs during 1a and the planner confirms):

1. More than one parcel, or parcels in more than one jurisdiction.
2. Mapped waters, wetlands, flood hazard, or listed-species habitat on or adjacent to the site, so that the resource-permit stack is in play.
3. The stated use needs a discretionary entitlement (rezone, general plan amendment, specific plan amendment, use permit on a contested use) or annexation.
4. The client needs a package for a board, an investment committee, a lender, or a public agency, whether or not the site is complex.

**What the extra $2,500 and three staff hours buy, beyond the 1b scope:**

| Addition | Who | Hours | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sub-consultant desk read | Senior biologist, or engineer where infrastructure or flood is the issue | 2 | A specialist reads the 1a constraints and the planner's path and writes a half-page opinion: what is likely real, what is likely mapped noise, what field work would settle it, and the season window that governs it |
| Scenario comparison | Senior planner (within the 4) | included | Two or three development scenarios (the client's program, the HBU alternative, a by-right fallback) run through the model side by side: approvals, schedule, cost range, confidence, for each |
| Board-ready summary | Senior planner (within the 4) | included | A two-page summary and a ten-slide deck in the client's format: the decision, the scenarios, the five assumptions that matter, the verification plan and its cost |
| Second session | Principal | 1 | A presentation to the board or committee, with questions, in place of a memo they read alone |
| Multi-parcel handling | Model | — | Each parcel screened, then the assemblage read as one site, with the jurisdiction split shown |

What it is not: no field work, no agency meetings, no studies, no pro forma. Those are Step 2 and later, and the 1b Plus scope says so.

## The client dashboard tie

From Step 1a the client has a dashboard link (the existing Client Links page on the Cox dashboard, extended). It shows the current roadmap (approvals, schedule, season windows), the program budget with confidence by line, the assumption register with what is verified and what is open, the change log, and the next step with its price. Every model refresh republishes it. At Step 2 and later the same page becomes the client statement already in use (hours, invoiced, paid, percent complete by deliverable), so the client sees the plan and the money on one page. The model writes one JSON per project into the dashboard's data folder the same way the budgets snapshot does today.

## What "inside the project coordination fee" means in a scope

Every task order's coordination line reads, in substance: *Project coordination (10% of labor) includes maintaining the project Roadmap model: monthly schedule and budget refresh, assumption register updates as deliverables land, change notices for rule or map changes affecting the site, and the client dashboard and statement.* That sentence makes the model a billed, expected part of every project rather than a free extra.

## Rules

1. One price per depth, no credits. A free launch screen is a 1a; the client who proceeds pays the full 1b.
2. A second site for the same client is a new 1a unless it is part of the same assemblage.
3. Re-screening a stale 1a (more than 12 months old, or the jurisdiction changed its rules) is a new 1a.
4. No fee ever re-establishes a fact the model already holds. If a later task order would re-do work, the register says so and the scope excludes it.
5. The 1b always ends with a priced Step 2 proposal; the proposal is part of the $2,500, not a separate sales step.
6. The market section ships in every 1a, including the free ones.
